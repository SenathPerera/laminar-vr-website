01.01 / PROJECT OVERVIEWR26-DS-006
RESEARCH ABSTRACT

The big picture.

Laminar VR is a wellness research prototype that investigates how wearable-informed artificial intelligence can personalize a stationary virtual reality meditation experience. A mobile application relays photoplethysmography (PPG) from a wearable to a real-time stress-inference service, while the Meta Quest 2 experience uses validated stress, signal quality, session context, and participant preferences to guide safe visual and audio adaptation.

The project brings together four research contributions: causal four-level stress inference, a safety-constrained adaptive visual environment, confidence-aware personalized meditation audio, and pre-session/post-session voice validation. The study asks whether these components can operate as one traceable system while remaining gradual, uncertainty-aware, and suitable for short meditation sessions. It is not a diagnostic or treatment system.

01.02 / BACKGROUND

Literature survey.

Immersive relaxation and biofeedback. Prior work reviewed by the team suggests that immersive natural environments and physiological biofeedback can support relaxation, but user comfort, sensory preference, and susceptibility to cybersickness vary. This motivates a stationary experience whose atmosphere changes gradually instead of relying on one fixed setting for every participant.

Physiological stress inference. Heart-rate variability (HRV) derived from wearable PPG can provide a useful view of autonomic response, yet real-world measurements are noisy and individual baselines drift over time. A deployable model must therefore be causal, preserve signal-quality information, and represent uncertainty instead of forcing every window into an overconfident label.

Adaptive visual and auditory environments. Reinforcement learning and contextual bandits can personalize environmental decisions from feedback, but short sessions provide sparse evidence and leave little room for unsafe exploration. The reviewed literature therefore supports preference-based initialization, bounded actions, gradual transitions, conservative fallbacks, and explicit comparison with static or rule-based baselines.

Multimodal outcome evaluation. Voice carries affective and prosodic information that is different from HRV. Comparing pre-session and post-session voice estimates with physiological evidence can reveal agreement, uncertainty, or possible mismatch, while confidence gating prevents a weak voice estimate from overruling stronger physiological evidence.

Source material: Stress inference proposal, adaptive VR proposal, adaptive audio proposal, and voice validation proposal. See their reference lists for the original studies.

01.03 / MOTIVATION

Research gap.

The project addresses a system-level gap between promising individual techniques and a usable adaptive meditation workflow. Stress inference, VR adaptation, audio personalization, and outcome validation are often evaluated separately; integrating them introduces causal, safety, uncertainty, interoperability, and evidence-attribution problems that isolated models do not solve.

GAP / 01

Causal, trustworthy input

Offline physiological models can use information unavailable at runtime. Live adaptation needs past-only inference, fresh windows, signal-quality checks, and uncertainty-aware outputs.

GAP / 02

Safe personalization

A policy must learn from limited session feedback without abrupt sensory changes, unsafe exploration, camera manipulation, or reactions to stale data.

GAP / 03

Coordinated adaptation

Visual and audio systems share physiological context but require separate policies, safety boundaries, rewards, and logs so their effects remain interpretable.

GAP / 04

Outcome validation

A single modality can be ambiguous. Pre/post voice analysis, HRV comparison, self-report, and anomaly checks provide complementary evidence without treating any one signal as ground truth.

Integrated research opportunity: Evaluate a traceable end-to-end prototype in which wearable acquisition, causal stress inference, independent visual and audio adaptation, session controls, and multimodal validation exchange versioned data while failing safely when evidence is missing or unreliable.

01.04 / CORE QUESTION

Research problem.

Fixed meditation experiences cannot reflect every participant's preferences or changing state, while automated adaptation can become counterproductive when it reacts to noisy physiology, changes too quickly, or optimizes a signal without validating the broader session outcome. The research problem is therefore both predictive and interactive: infer stress responsibly, act conservatively, and preserve enough evidence to explain the response.

How can a wearable-informed, safety-constrained VR meditation system personalize visual and auditory conditions from uncertain physiological feedback while reliably evaluating stress-related change across the session?

The question unifies the four component studies without collapsing their separate hypotheses, policies, safety rules, and evaluation criteria.

01.05 / RESEARCH INTENT

Research objectives.

Main objective

To design, integrate, and evaluate a wellness research prototype that uses wearable physiology and participant context to deliver safe, personalized VR meditation and assess immediate stress-related change through complementary measures.

Specific objectives

  1. Build a wearable-to-mobile acquisition path that transports real PPG samples without presenting generated values as measured physiology.
  2. Develop causal four-level stress inference that combines engineered HRV features with sequence modeling and communicates confidence, signal quality, and continuous stress context.
  3. Evaluate a preference-initialized adaptive visual environment whose five normalized atmosphere dimensions are controlled by static, rule-based, or contextual-bandit study policies behind deterministic safety checks.
  4. Develop confidence-aware adaptive meditation audio using participant preferences, a trained PPO policy, bounded parameter changes, stable mixing, and generated meditation clips.
  5. Assess sessions through a voice-based validation pipeline that checks recording quality, compares pre/post stress estimates, cross-checks HRV, and identifies anomalous patterns.
  6. Integrate the components through versioned contracts, transport-neutral interfaces, and reconstructable telemetry, then evaluate model quality, safety, responsiveness, comfort, and preliminary immediate outcomes.
01.06 / RESEARCH DESIGN

Research methodology.

The project follows an iterative design-science and experimental approach. Each researcher owns a distinct artifact and evaluation question, while shared mobile, wearable, networking, and data-contract layers allow the components to operate together without duplicating physiological logic.

01

Define

Review prior work, define component hypotheses, establish safety constraints, and agree on shared data contracts.

02

Build

Implement the wearable/mobile path, causal stress service, Quest environment, audio agent, and voice-validation service.

03

Verify

Use deterministic unit, integration, parity, schema, and Unity runtime tests to verify component behavior and failure handling.

04

Integrate

Connect session setup, physiology, Quest controls, visual/audio outputs, telemetry, and pre/post validation through explicit interfaces.

05

Evaluate

Compare component baselines, perform device checks, and conduct a consent-based preliminary pilot using paired outcomes and comfort monitoring.

Evaluation dimensions: Component B is assessed through ordinal classification, causality, parity, and live inference behavior; the visual component through policy baselines, safety, valid reward attribution, Quest performance, physiology, and self-report; audio through safe-control behavior, stability, personalization, and latency; and Component D through language-aware voice scoring, pre/post change, cross-modal agreement, confidence handling, and anomaly detection. Integrated human findings will be reported as preliminary rather than proof of general effectiveness.

WEARABLE PPG→MOBILE + STRESS INFERENCE→ADAPTIVE VR VISUAL + AUDIO→PRE/POST VALIDATION

The mobile application also prepares sessions, collects preferences and context, and carries session controls. Voice and questionnaire evidence is collected around the VR experience, so the strip shows dependencies rather than an exact linear runtime sequence.

01.07 / IMPLEMENTATION FRAMEWORK

Core technologies.

The current prototype combines embedded sensing, mobile orchestration, local services, standalone XR, machine learning, and generative audio behind explicit contracts and safety boundaries.

WEARABLE + MOBILE + CONTRACTS

ESP32-S3 / MAX30100 / React Native

A MAX30100 PPG sensor streams timestamped samples over BLE to a React Native and TypeScript mobile application. WebSockets carry live research data, JSON Schema defines session configuration, and Supabase supports authenticated participant and session records.

STRESS INFERENCE

Python / FastAPI / HRV ML

A causal two-way ensemble blends XGBoost over engineered features with a Multi-Scale Circadian-Guided Cross-Attention sequence model, using past-only baselines and confidence-gated four-level output.

ADAPTIVE VISUALS

Unity 6 / C# / OpenXR

A standalone Meta Quest 2 application uses URP, OpenXR, ScriptableObject configuration, WebSocket adapters, deterministic safety validation, and a lightweight LinUCB contextual-bandit policy.

ADAPTIVE AUDIO

PPO / Unity audio / Google Lyria

A Stable-Baselines3 PPO actor is exported for direct Unity inference. A confidence-aware safety filter bounds seven audio controls, while a Python backend requests personalized Lyria-generated meditation clips.

VOICE VALIDATION

emotion2vec+ / prosody / VAE

A Python and FastAPI pipeline combines recording-quality checks, a frozen emotion2vec+ encoder, prosodic features, pre/post comparison, HRV cross-checking, and a per-user variational autoencoder for anomaly detection.

01.08 / INDIVIDUAL CONTRIBUTIONS

Four research components.

The mobile application, wearable, session relay, and shared contracts connect the four contributions; they are enabling infrastructure rather than an additional individual research component.

◎
COMMON LAYER / SHARED INFRASTRUCTURE

Wearable, mobile, session, and data backbone

The ESP32-S3 wearable supplies raw PPG; the mobile application handles BLE transport, preferences, context, session preparation, and controls; shared schemas and WebSocket services connect the mobile, local AI services, and Quest while preserving component boundaries.

COMPONENT / 01Cosinor-enhanced multi-level stress inference from wearable RR intervals under real-time deployment ConstraintsB.D.P.D. Perera · IT22191038+

Research focus: Infer relaxed, mild, moderate, or high stress from wearable-derived HRV while remaining causal, uncertainty-aware, and suitable for live use.

Implemented approach: A 60-beat ensemble combines XGBoost features with a Multi-Scale Circadian-Guided Cross-Attention model. Past-only EWMA baselines prevent future leakage, and low-margin predictions are returned as adjacent-level bands rather than false precision.

Evaluation: Nested model evaluation, ordinal metrics, causality checks, batch/streaming parity, signal-quality handling, and live WebSocket inference. Consumers receive heart rate, RMSSD, SDNN, four-class probabilities, a continuous score, and the authoritative confidence-gated decision.

COMPONENT / 02Safety-constrained adaptive VR environmentW.T.S.R. Perera · IT22231314+

Research focus: Personalize the atmosphere of a stationary Japanese Temple Pond Garden on Meta Quest 2 using participant preferences, session context, and validated physiological feedback.

Implemented approach: Unity represents illumination, warmth, atmospheric softness, color richness, and ambient motion as normalized dimensions. Static-personalized, rule-based, and LinUCB contextual-bandit policies propose one small change at a time; every action passes through deterministic safety validation and a smooth transition layer.

Evaluation: Compare study policies using valid pre/post action windows, physiological reward evidence, self-report, comfort, decision traceability, and on-device performance. The MVP makes no cross-scene generalization claim.

View individual proposal (PDF) ↗
COMPONENT / 03Confidence-aware personalized adaptive audio controllerK.S.M. Samarasekara · IT22172396+

Research focus: Personalize meditation audio from long-term preferences and live physiological context while avoiding abrupt or low-confidence reactions.

Implemented approach: A directly exported PPO actor proposes bounded residual changes to seven audio parameters. A separate safety filter freezes, dampens, or recovers control according to confidence, signal quality, staleness, and emergency state; Unity applies smooth playback and requests context-matched Lyria clips through a local backend.

Evaluation: Compare the safe rule baseline with PPO-residual control using delayed, non-overlapping physiology rewards, preference alignment, stability, novelty, comfort, and latency. Transitions are logged for offline analysis; PPO is not retrained during a Unity session.

View individual proposal (PDF) ↗
COMPONENT / 04Multilingual voice-based stress assessment with cross-modal session validationS. Prathikesh · IT22621788+

Research focus: Use conversational voice before and after meditation as an independent view of stress-related change, then interpret it alongside Component B's physiological estimate.

Implemented approach: A five-layer pipeline performs recording-quality checks, combines a frozen emotion2vec+ representation with prosodic features, compares confidence-weighted pre/post scores, checks voice/HRV agreement, and applies a per-user variational autoencoder for anomaly detection.

Evaluation: English voice performance, stress-change direction, confidence-gated cross-modal agreement, and anomalous-session detection are evaluated explicitly. Sinhala is a limited zero-shot condition and Tamil remains future evaluation, so multilingual capability is not overstated.
